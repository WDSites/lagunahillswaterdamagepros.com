Choosing a Water Damage Drying Company in Laguna Hills, CA

A good drying company shows its license, explains its equipment and hands you daily moisture readings. A weak one shows up with a few fans and a verbal promise.

What to check before you sign anything

Hire a drying company the way you would hire any contractor, with proof before trust. Check the California contractor license, confirm the deposit is legal, and ask how the crew proves the structure is dry before it leaves.

The pressure is real. The EPA says wet areas should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold, so many people sign the first paper put in front of them. A five-minute check on the phone costs less than a bad contract.

Hiring checklist

Ask these in order. A crew that gets annoyed by them is a warning on its own.

  • Is the contractor licensed with the California Contractors State License Board, and is the status active at cslb.ca.gov?
  • Is the quote in writing, with a scope that names the rooms, the materials to be removed and the equipment to be placed?
  • Will the crew work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration? Ask any company whether its crews follow it.
  • What equipment goes in: air movers, LGR dehumidifiers, a thermal imaging camera and moisture meters? How many of each for your rooms?
  • Who comes back each day to check the readings, and are they written down?
  • What reading ends the job, and who signs off that the structure is dry?
  • How does the company identify the water category (clean, gray or black) before choosing what to remove?
  • What happens if drying runs longer than quoted, and how is that approved?
  • Will you get photos of every wet area before and after, for your insurer?

What the California license rules say

License at $1,000

CSLB requires a license for work worth $1,000 or more in labor and materials, and for any job that needs a permit.

Down payment cap

A down payment is capped at the lesser of 10 percent or $1,000. The exception is a contractor with a blanket performance and payment bond.

Three-day cancel

You generally have three days to cancel in writing, and five days if you are a senior. Read the notice before you sign.

Verify it yourself

Look up the license at cslb.ca.gov or call (800) 321-2752. Do not rely on a business card or a logo on a truck.

Red flags during the first visit

  • No license number on the contract, or a number that does not match the company name at cslb.ca.gov.
  • A request for cash, or a deposit larger than the lesser of 10 percent or $1,000.
  • Fans placed with no meter readings taken, and no dehumidifier on a closed-up house.
  • A flat promise of how many days drying will take before anyone has scanned the walls and floor.
  • Pressure to tear out everything right away without showing you moisture readings.
  • No mention of water category, even though gray or black water changes what must be removed.

Honest limits

A drying crew dries. It does not decide if your insurer pays, and it cannot promise a drying time before it sees the wet areas. Be wary of anyone who names a fixed day count over the phone.

The rebuild is a separate stage. Once the readings are dry, water damage cleanup and repair crews handle what had to be removed. We do not handle plumbing repairs or roof work.
