How storm water gets inside during and after the rain

Storm water damage in Laguna Hills usually begins while the rain is still falling and gets worse in the hours after it ends. Water enters at the weak points of a house, soaks whatever it touches, and keeps wicking into framing and flooring long after the sky clears.

Four common entry points

Windows and doors

Wind pushes rain sideways past worn weatherstripping, failed caulk and low door thresholds, then it runs down the wall cavity or across the floor.

Gutters and downspouts

Clogged or undersized gutters overflow at the eave, and downspouts that dump next to the slab send water straight toward the foundation.

Garage door and foundation

Runoff pools against the garage door seal or the stem wall and pushes under the door or through cracks at the slab edge.

Slopes and drains

Water sheeting off a hillside or a paved yard can outrun a small yard drain, then back up toward the house.

Is this a storm leak, a flood or a roof leak?

This page covers water that gets in because of the storm itself: wind-driven rain, overflowing gutters and runoff that reaches the house. If creek water or rising floodwater is entering living space, the job belongs under flood damage restoration, which deals with deeper water and heavier contamination.

If the water drips from a ceiling because shingles, tile or flashing failed, that is a roofing problem first, covered on the roof leak water damage page.

Why storm water is rarely clean

Rain that falls straight into a room is clean at first. Once it crosses a yard, a driveway or a street, it picks up soil, fertilizer, pet waste and oil. Based on IICRC S500 water categories, ground-surface water is treated as Category 3, and water that is merely questionable is treated as Category 2 until proven otherwise.

That matters for porous materials. Carpet, pad, insulation and cardboard that soaked up Category 2 or 3 water are usually removed, not cleaned. Category 2 water left standing for more than 48 hours can turn into Category 3, so the clock matters here.

Garages and ground-floor rooms take the worst of it. Most homes here have no basement, so garages, crawl spaces and slab-on-grade rooms carry the risk, and stored cardboard wicks water upward.

Safety first: the first hour after storm water enters

  1. Get people and pets clear

    Keep everyone out of standing water that crossed the yard.

  2. Check the panel before anything else

    Cut power to wet rooms at the panel only if the floor around it is dry. Otherwise call 911 or your utility.

  3. Stop more water entering

    Clear a blocked downspout, place towels or sandbags at a door threshold, and close the garage door if runoff is pushing in.

  4. Photograph before moving things

    Take wide shots and close-ups of the water line, the entry point and damaged items.

What the crew does on a storm water job

  1. Scan and map

    A technician uses moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to find wet walls, floors and cavities, including spots that look dry.

  2. Extract standing water

    Extraction units and wet vacuums remove water from carpet, slab and low spots before drying starts.

  3. Remove what cannot be saved

    Pad, insulation and cardboard that took on contaminated water are bagged and hauled.

  4. Dry the structure

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings match a dry reference. See water mitigation.

  5. Hand off the source fix

    We do not repair windows, gutters, grading or drains. A licensed trade fixes those.

Document storm damage the way an adjuster will want it

  • Photograph the entry point from inside and outside, with the date visible.
  • Shoot the water line on walls and furniture with a tape measure beside it.
  • Keep every receipt for emergency steps, such as sandbags, a rented pump or a hotel night.
  • Wait for the adjuster before permanent repairs, and ask before discarding large items.

Storm water and your insurance policy

The California Department of Insurance says homeowners policies generally exclude flood, and a separate flood policy through the National Flood Insurance Program takes effect 30 days after signing. Rain that enters through a damaged roof or window is a different question from rising water, so read your own policy and ask your insurer which category your loss falls in.

Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address, since the city code adopts FEMA's Flood Insurance Study dated August 22, 2008 and flood maps dated December 3, 2009. We do not decide coverage.

Mistakes that make storm damage worse

  • Walking through the water barefoot or in street shoes you will wear again.
  • Running a household vacuum on a wet floor.
  • Waiting a day to see whether the carpet dries on its own.
  • Pushing water toward a wall with a broom instead of extracting it.
