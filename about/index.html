What this site is

Laguna Hills Water Damage Pros connects homeowners and property managers in Laguna Hills and the surrounding cities with licensed and insured restoration crews. You call one number, describe the damage, and dispatch arranges the next step.

We explain the scope and how the price is set before work starts, so you can ask questions first. We do not promise an arrival time, and we do not decide insurance coverage.

What dispatch needs from you

  • Your street address and the nearest cross street.
  • Where the water came from, if you know, and whether it has been shut off.
  • Which rooms are wet and what material is wet: carpet, hardwood, drywall, ceiling.
  • Whether the water might be sewage or storm water.
  • Whether anyone else, such as a neighbor or an HOA, is affected.

What we do and what we do not

We handle water mitigation, emergency water removal, cleanup, repair, flood damage and mold remediation. We do not do roofing, plumbing repair, waterproofing, foundation work or legal advice.

When another trade is the real fix, we say so. A leak that is never repaired will damage the room again, no matter how well it was dried.

How to check any contractor

Verify a contractor's license at cslb.ca.gov or by calling (800) 321-2752. California requires a license for work of $1,000 or more, and caps the down payment at the lesser of 10 percent or $1,000. You also have a three-day right to cancel a home improvement contract in writing, and five days if you are a senior.

Ask whether crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage and S520 for mold. Ask for the scope in writing, ask how drying will be verified, and keep copies of every moisture log and photo.

How a typical job runs from call to closeout

  1. The call

    Dispatch asks where the water came from, what is wet and whether anyone is at risk. You get plain first steps, such as shutting off the main valve or staying out of contaminated water.

  2. The assessment

    A technician checks moisture with meters and a thermal imaging camera, then explains the scope and how the price is set before any work starts.

  3. The dry-out and cleanup

    Water is extracted, wet materials that cannot dry are removed, and air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach the dry goal.

  4. The records

    Moisture logs, equipment lists and dated photos are kept so your insurer and any repair contractor can see what happened and what was done.

Standards and agencies we point people to

Two published industry standards guide this work. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers water damage restoration, including the three water categories, and S520 covers mold remediation. We name them so you can ask any company whether its crews follow them.

For insurance questions, the California Department of Insurance publishes consumer guides, and its hotline is 800-927-4357. For contractor checks, the Contractors State License Board lists license status online. For mold and moisture basics, the EPA and the California Department of Public Health publish free guides.

Questions worth asking before you agree to work

  • What is the written scope, and how is the price set?
  • How will drying be verified, and what reading ends the job?
  • Who removes materials, and what happens to my belongings?
  • Will you document the loss for my insurance claim, with photos and moisture logs?
  • What does the contract say about changes, deposits and cancellation?

Why these pages cite public agencies

Water damage is stressful, and plenty of online advice contradicts itself. Where this site gives a number or a rule, it comes from a city, county, state or federal source, and the guides name that source so you can check it yourself.

When a fact cannot be confirmed, we leave it out or send you to the right office. For example, flood zones are address-specific, so the pages point you to the FEMA Flood Map Service Center instead of guessing. Permit rules vary by scope, so they point you to the city building department.

If you spot something that is out of date, email contact@lagunahillswaterdamagepros.com and we will review the page.
