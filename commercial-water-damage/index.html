A wet suite is a different kind of job

Commercial water damage restoration in Laguna Hills covers a leak, burst line, sprinkler discharge, drain failure or roof leak that wets a business property. The crew extracts the water, removes wet materials, dries the structure and keeps the records you will need. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry reference for water categories and drying goals, and we cite it as a reference only.

We do not repair roofs, sprinklers or plumbing. A licensed trade fixes the source, and the crew works around that visit. The owner, the property manager and the insurer all have a stake, which is what makes the job different from a house. For the first hours, see emergency water removal.

Safety first: the first hour in a commercial space

  1. Call the owner or property manager

    They know where the suite and building shutoffs are and who holds the keys. Your water bill names your district.

  2. Shut off the water you can reach

    Close the valve serving the fixture or the suite. Do not shut down a fire sprinkler system on your own. Call 911 for a fire, and call the building's fire protection contractor for a discharging sprinkler.

  3. Respect the electrical panel

    Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it from a dry floor. If water has reached outlets, panels or lighting, stay clear and call 911 or the utility.

  4. Photograph and then call

    Take wide and close photos of the damage, the source and any equipment or inventory at risk. Then call (949) 670-0939.

Who signs off on the work

Authority depends on the building and the lease, so sort it out early.

Owner or landlord

Often controls the building shell, the roof and common lines, and may need to approve work that touches them.

Tenant

Lease terms usually say who is responsible for interior finishes, contents and tenant improvements. Read the lease before assuming.

Property manager

Commonly the contact for access, building hours, vendors and notices to other suites.

Shared walls, shared ceilings

Water ignores suite lines. It moves along a shared wall or drops through a ceiling cavity into the next unit. With access, the crew checks adjoining suites using a thermal imaging camera and moisture meter, and records each suite's readings separately.

Tell the property manager so neighbors can be warned. The landlord may control the ceiling space above a unit, which affects who allows the opening.

After-hours access and who holds the keys

Dispatch answers 24/7, and a crew can be sent same-day when available. Business sites add hurdles: alarm codes, building hours, dock rules and a key holder who is not on site.

Keep a contact reachable by phone, and tell dispatch about limits on noise or parking. Crews serve Lake Forest and Aliso Viejo properties from the same line.

What the crew actually does on site

  1. Water extraction

    Extraction units pull standing water from carpet, tile and low spots.

  2. Removal of wet materials

    Wet flooring, ceiling tile, drywall and insulation that cannot dry in place are cut out and bagged.

  3. Structural drying

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are set on the floor and in wall cavities. The structural drying page explains how placement is planned.

  4. Drying logs and moisture records

    Moisture meter readings and equipment locations are written down at each visit and kept as drying logs for the owner or insurer.

  5. Odor control and mold watch

    A HEPA air scrubber can run when dust or smell is an issue, and the crew watches for signs of mold.

Insurance, flood maps and your notes

Commercial property policies differ widely, so ask your agent what yours covers, including business interruption and any flood exclusion. The California Department of Insurance advises prompt reporting, reasonable steps to limit further damage, photographs, receipts, and waiting for the adjuster before permanent repairs.

California regulation 10 CCR 2695.7 sets 40 calendar days for an insurer to accept or deny after proof of claim and 30 to pay after acceptance. Ask your agent how it applies.

Flood from outside is a separate question. The City of Laguna Hills code adopts a FEMA Flood Insurance Study dated August 22, 2008 and flood maps dated December 3, 2009, so check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your property address.

Licenses and permits for the rebuild

The CSLB says a contractor license is required for work of $1,000 or more in labor and materials, and for any job that needs a permit. Verify any contractor at cslb.ca.gov or by calling (800) 321-2752 before signing.

Permit needs vary by scope. Ask Building and Safety at the Civic Center, (949) 707-2600, before a rebuild starts.

Mistakes that cost a property owner time

  • Waiting until Monday to report a weekend leak. Materials stay wet while you wait.
  • Throwing out damaged ceiling tile or carpet before photographing it.
  • Assuming the landlord or the tenant is responsible without checking the lease.
  • Forgetting that a quiet year does not clear a roof drain. The National Weather Service water-year normal is 11.18 inches at John Wayne Airport, a nearby station, so a roof that rarely gets tested should be checked before winter storms.
