Why the kitchen needs its own drying plan

Kitchen water damage restoration in Laguna Hills is about a room that packs a sink, a dishwasher, a refrigerator line, cabinets, a countertop and a floor into a few hundred square feet. Water from any one of them spreads into the others. A small leak at the sink base can end up under the flooring three feet away.

Many Laguna Hills kitchens have been remodeled at least once, since the Moulton Ranch was subdivided in the early 1960s. A fair question to ask is how many layers of floor, backsplash and cabinet finish sit over the original, because layered finishes can hide an old leak. Ask the crew to probe those layers with a moisture meter.

For leaks that start in a specific appliance, see appliance leak water damage. This page covers the room itself.

Where water hides in a kitchen

Sink base and supply lines

The cabinet floor soaks first, then water follows the pipe penetrations into the wall and subfloor.

Behind the dishwasher and refrigerator

Water collects in a space you cannot see. A pull-out check and a thermal imaging scan find what the eye misses.

Toe kicks and cabinet bases

The toe kick is the first board to swell, and it signals water in the box above or the floor beneath.

Under flooring and tile

Vinyl and laminate trap water against the subfloor. Tile hides moisture in the bed and in the plywood below it.

Island cabinets

Plumbing for an island sink or a dishwasher often runs through the slab or floor, and leaks stay hidden longer there.

Toe kick swelling?

Swollen cabinet bases mean water has been sitting. Call before the countertop is ordered.

Safety first: the first hour

  1. Shut off the water

    Close the angle stop under the sink or the appliance valve. If that does not stop it, close the main valve.

  2. Turn off power only from a dry panel

    Kitchens have outlets at counter height and appliance circuits. If water is near them or the panel is wet, call 911 or your utility.

  3. Empty the lower cabinets

    Move cleaners and food out of the wet base so nothing soaks up water and so the crew can see the cabinet floor.

  4. Photograph and mop standing water

    Take photos before you move anything. Then call (949) 670-0939 and describe what is swollen or soft.

The drying order inside a kitchen

A crew starts with extraction, since standing water on tile or vinyl is quickest to remove. Next come the hidden cavities: behind appliances, under the sink base and below the toe kicks, where water sits longest. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras map them before any wall is opened.

Cabinets are dried in place when the boxes are plywood and the finish is intact. A small hole in the toe kick lets air movers push warm air into the base. LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room, and readings are logged each day until they match a dry reference. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry reference for water categories and drying goals, and we cite it as a reference only.

Cabinets come out when particleboard has swelled, when the base is soft or when the subfloor underneath needs drying and cannot be reached otherwise. Removing them is cheaper than a repeat failure.

Countertops and tile

Stone and quartz do not absorb water the way cabinets do, so the concern is what is below them. Laminate counters with a particleboard core can swell at seams. Tile counters rest on a mortar bed, and moisture there is checked before anything is replaced.

Tile floors are tougher than the plywood beneath them. A crew checks the subfloor from below or through a small opening, because water that gets under grout lines has nowhere to go.

Before cabinet or countertop replacement

  • The leak source is fixed by a licensed plumber and checked for drips
  • Subfloor and wall framing read dry on a moisture meter
  • Wet insulation, damaged board and swollen base cabinets have been removed
  • Your insurer has seen photos and, if a claim is open, the adjuster has approved the plan
  • You have asked the city whether a permit applies (Civic Center Building and Safety, (949) 707-2600)

Replacing a counter over a wet cabinet base locks moisture in. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours, so start early.

What changes the cost

How many cabinets are wet

A single sink base is a small job. A run of cabinets with a wet floor under them is a larger one.

Cabinet material and finish

Plywood boxes dry in place more often than particleboard, and custom doors cost more to match.

Flooring layers

Tile, vinyl and hardwood come up differently, and more layers means more work.

Time since the leak

Water found the same day dries faster than water found after a week.

Water quality, scale and your fittings

The El Toro Water District 2025 water quality report lists imported treated water at 270 mg/L hardness, about 16 grains per gallon. Water that hard leaves mineral scale on faucets, angle stops and supply fittings over time. We do not claim scale causes leaks, but crusty fittings are worth showing your plumber.

Water district boundaries are not published by address, so ask your water district which one serves you.

Insurance, licensing and paperwork

Policies typically cover sudden kitchen leaks more readily than long-term seepage, so read your own policy. Photograph the damage, keep receipts and wait for the adjuster before permanent repairs.

The CSLB requires a license for work of $1,000 or more in labor and materials and for any job needing a permit. A down payment is capped at the lesser of 10 percent or $1,000. We do not decide coverage and we do not bill insurers directly.
