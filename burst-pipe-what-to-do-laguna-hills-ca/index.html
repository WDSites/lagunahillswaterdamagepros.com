Burst Pipe in Laguna Hills, CA: What to Do in the First Hour

The first sixty minutes decide how far the water spreads. Shut the supply, protect the electrical, then get drying started.

Do these three things right now

First, shut off the water. Every minute of flow soaks more drywall, flooring and insulation. Second, if water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, treat the electrical as the danger. Third, call someone who can pull the water out.

A plumber fixes the pipe. A restoration crew removes the water and dries the structure. You usually need both, and you do not need to wait for the first before calling the second.

The first-hour step list

It is okay to feel rushed. Work through these in order and take them one at a time.

  1. Close the main shutoff

    Turn the main valve clockwise until it stops. Ball valves with a lever turn a quarter turn, so the handle sits crosswise to the pipe when closed. Many homes have the main near the street, at the front of the house, or in the garage.

  2. Open the lowest faucet and flush toilets once

    Opening a tap on the lowest level drains the pipes and slows the leak. Flush each toilet once to empty the tanks, then leave the taps open.

  3. Check the electrical before you walk in

    If water is pooling around outlets or the panel, do not step into it. If the panel is in a dry spot, shut off power to the wet rooms. If you cannot reach it safely, call 911 or the utility.

  4. Stop the spread

    Roll up rugs, lift furniture onto blocks, pull soaked items out of cabinets, and put towels along doorways. Keep children and pets out of the wet area.

  5. Photograph and video the damage

    Take wide and close-up shots of the wet ceiling, walls, floors and the burst section. Keep the broken piece if the plumber removes it.

  6. Call your insurer and a drying crew

    Report the loss and ask for a claim number. Then call dispatch so extraction, air movers and dehumidifiers can start before moisture reaches the framing.

If you are unsure what the water has touched, assume it has reached more than you can see. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show wet drywall and subfloor behind paint that still looks dry.

Why burst pipes are not always dramatic

Not every failure sprays water across a room. A pinhole in a copper line, a cracked fitting under a sink or a loose joint inside a wall can drip for days. A water bill that jumps with no change in habits, a musty smell in a closet, or a stain that keeps returning are all worth a closer look.

If you suspect a hidden leak, close the main and watch the meter. A dial that keeps moving with every fixture off points to a leak somewhere between the meter and the house. A plumber can pressure test the lines, and a thermal imaging camera can show which wall the water is traveling through.

What a crew does in the first hours

Crews start with a walk-through and moisture readings, then use extraction units to pull standing water. Wet carpet pad is usually discarded, and wet baseboards may be pulled to let walls breathe.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then run for several days, with readings logged each visit. Typical drying for a minor leak is 1 to 3 days, though larger losses take longer. The EPA advises drying within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold, which is why the first call matters more than the first bucket.

What to tell the crew on the phone

  • Where the pipe failed and whether the water is now shut off
  • Which rooms and floors are wet, and whether any ceilings are bulging
  • Floor types: carpet, hardwood, tile, laminate or vinyl
  • Whether the electrical panel or any outlets got wet
  • How long the water ran before you caught it
  • Your insurer and claim number, if you already have one
