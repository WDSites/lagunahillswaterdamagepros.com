What a slab leak is and why it floods a room

Slab leak water damage in Laguna Hills starts when a supply or drain line cracks under or inside the concrete foundation. Many homes here sit on slab foundations, so the pipes that feed kitchens, baths and water heaters often run beneath the floor instead of through a crawl space.

Water from a leaking line has nowhere to go but up and out. It finds seams, cracks and the edges where flooring meets the slab, then spreads under carpet, laminate or tile and wicks into the bottom of the walls.

The city's adopted Safety Element lists expansive soils in Laguna Hills that can cause minor to severe structural damage. That does not tell you why any one pipe leaked, but it is a reason to photograph new slab cracks and floor movement and mention them to the plumber.

Safety first: the first hour

  1. Shut off the main

    Close the main water valve at the house, usually at the front near the meter or at a hose bib line. Then check whether the meter dial still moves.

  2. Keep power away from wet floors

    If water is near outlets or a panel, cut breakers only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. Otherwise stay out and call 911 or your electric utility.

  3. Call a licensed plumber

    Ask for leak detection first. The plumber locates the line and quotes a spot repair or a repipe. We do not repair plumbing.

  4. Photograph before anything moves

    Shoot the meter, the wet areas, the baseboards and any cracks.

  5. Call dispatch about the wet side

    Tell the 24/7 line which rooms are wet. The crew extracts standing water and removes flooring that cannot dry.

Slab leak or groundwater? Run the meter test

Water on a slab is not always a pipe. The Safety Element describes shallow groundwater, about 5 to 10 feet down, along Sulphur, Aliso and Oso Creeks, and a surface water source such as a bad downspout or irrigation can also seep toward a foundation.

Turn off the main and watch the meter for 15 to 30 minutes with every fixture and appliance off. If the dial keeps moving, the leak is in a pressurized line. If it holds still and the floor stays damp, ask the plumber to check for an outside water source before anyone opens the slab.

Who does what on a slab leak job

The licensed plumber

Finds the leak with acoustic or thermal detection, then repairs the line in place, reroutes it, or repipes. This is plumbing work and it is not ours.

The restoration crew

Drying and monitoring

You

Why a slab cannot be rushed dry

Concrete holds water and releases it slowly. A crew can dry the carpet, pad and wall bottoms in days, but the slab itself may keep giving off moisture for a good while afterward.

So the crew does not call the job done when the surface feels dry. Moisture meters on the slab, subfloor and wall bases are logged on every visit, and a thermal imaging camera helps spot cool, damp patches. Crews that follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published water damage standard, set drying goals this way, and flooring waits for those readings.

If baseboard or wall board stayed wet, a mold check happens before new material goes in. Mold needs moisture, so the plumber fixes the source first. Health questions belong to a physician.
