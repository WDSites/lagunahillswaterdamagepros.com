When standing water is an emergency

If water is pooling on the floor, dripping through a ceiling or rising from a drain, treat it as an emergency. This line is open 24/7, and emergency water removal in Laguna Hills starts with a call that tells the crew what kind of water, how much and where.

While you wait, stop the source if it is safe. Turn off the main water valve, lift valuables and electronics off the floor, and keep children and pets out of the water. If water touches an outlet or panel, stay out, cut power only if you can reach the panel dry, and call 911 or your utility before the restoration line.

Crews arrive with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, submersible pumps and wet vacuums. We do not promise an arrival time, but same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

How water extraction works

  1. Find and stop the source

    The crew confirms the shut-off, checks the supply line or drain that failed, and makes the area safe to enter.

  2. Pump and extract

    Submersible pumps move deep water. Extraction wands pull water from carpet, pad and hard flooring.

  3. Remove what cannot dry

    Soaked carpet pad, wet insulation and swollen baseboards come out so they cannot hold water against the structure.

  4. Hand off to drying

    Remaining moisture is mapped and drying equipment is set, which is the work of water mitigation.

Burst pipe water damage: the first hour

A burst supply line can release gallons per minute, and a pinhole leak inside a wall can run for days before a stain appears. Common culprits in older homes are corroded supply lines, failed fittings and water heater connections.

In the first hour, shut the main valve, open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain lines, and turn off the water heater if it is leaking. Photograph everything before you move it. Then call dispatch and ask the technician to meet you at the wettest room first.

Other emergencies we extract

Washing machine and appliance floods

A failed fill hose or dishwasher line can soak a whole floor, including the room below. Extraction starts in the source room and follows water into neighbors.

Toilet and tub overflows

Clean overflow is a Category 1 job. Overflow with waste is Category 2 or 3 and requires gloves, containment and disinfecting.

Roof and window leaks in storms

Winter storms can send rain through flashing and vents. Rain through a roof is typically clean at first, but it picks up dirt and contaminants as it moves.

Crawl space and garage water

Water that collects under a raised floor or in a garage needs pumps and a plan to dry what it touched.

Equipment, in plain words

Extraction units and submersible pumps move bulk water. Moisture meters show how wet a material really is. Thermal imaging cameras reveal cool, wet areas behind drywall. After extraction, air movers push air across surfaces and dehumidifiers pull water out of the air so the rest can evaporate.

Drying equipment comes after extraction, and renting it out without a crew is not part of this service. Technicians set it up, check it daily and remove it when readings say the structure is dry.

What affects the cost of emergency removal

Water category

Clean water costs less to handle than gray or black water.

Depth and area

More square feet and more depth mean more extraction time.

Materials involved

Carpet, pad, hardwood and drywall are handled differently.

Time since the loss

Water left overnight spreads, so the dry-out takes longer.
