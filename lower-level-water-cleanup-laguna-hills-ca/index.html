Lower-Level Water Cleanup After a Storm in Laguna Hills, CA

Storm water in a garage, sunken room or lower level needs a different plan than a pipe leak, starting with what kind of water it is.

What "lower level" means in Laguna Hills

Most Laguna Hills homes have no basement, so this guide covers the places storm water actually collects here: attached garages, sunken living rooms, split-level lower floors, laundry rooms at slab level, and the base of walls that meet a patio or planter.

Water gets into these spaces from outside. It comes under a door, through a foundation crack, across a garage threshold, or up from a floor drain. That outside origin matters because it changes how the cleanup is done.

Find the water category first

Based on IICRC S500 categories, restoration crews sort water by contamination level. The category sets the gear, the protective equipment and what can be saved.

CategoryWhat it meansTypical sourceWhat usually happens to materials
Category 1 (clean)Water from a sanitary sourceBroken supply line, clean rain through a roof leakOften dried in place if acted on quickly
Category 2 (gray)Significant contaminationWasher or dishwasher discharge, Category 1 left standing too longCarpet pad is usually removed, hard surfaces cleaned and dried
Category 3 (black)Grossly contaminatedSewage, ground-surface flood water entering from outsidePorous materials removed, structure cleaned and disinfected by trained crews

Storm water that crosses a yard, driveway or street is treated as Category 3. Assume that applies to your garage floor, even if the water looks clear. Category 2 water left over 48 hours may become Category 3, which is why the clock matters.

Safety before anything else

Do not step into standing water if outlets, power strips, a freezer or a garage door opener sit at or below the waterline. If you can reach your panel through dry floor, shut off power to the wet area. If you cannot, or if you smell gas, leave and call 911 from outside.

What you can do before the crew arrives

  1. Stop more water from coming in

    Move to higher ground first. Block door gaps with towels or sandbags if you have them, and clear leaves from drains outside only when conditions are safe.

  2. Protect yourself

    Wear rubber boots and waterproof gloves. Keep children, pets and anyone with a weakened immune system out of the water.

  3. Lift what you can

    Raise furniture legs onto blocks, pull rugs, and move boxes to shelves. Cardboard wicks water upward fast, so put paper items and photographs out of the area first.

  4. Photograph before you touch

    Capture the waterline on walls, the flooring and stored items. These photos help your insurer, and you will want them if a flood policy is involved.

  5. Open doors, skip the fans

    Fresh air helps, but the CDPH advises against running fans if more than 48 hours have passed since flooding because they can spread mold spores.

Extraction comes next. A crew uses truck-mounted or portable extraction units to pull water from slab and carpet, then sets air movers and LGR dehumidifiers and checks moisture meters daily until readings drop.

What the crew does that a wet vac cannot

A shop vacuum removes surface water. It cannot reach water that has wicked into the bottom of drywall, behind baseboards or under a floating floor. Thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters show where it went.

Drywall and insulation that have been wet more than 72 hours are generally removed. A flood cut, meaning a cut about two feet up the wall, exposes wet framing so it can dry. Carpet and pad contaminated by outside water are usually discarded.

Local note

Laguna Hills: where the city says water collects

The city's Safety Element notes 1 percent annual-chance flooding around Aliso Creek, Veeh Reservoir, Mill Creek and northwest of Alicia Parkway. If your home is near one of them, check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address and keep the sandbag or door-barrier question on your pre-storm list.

The same document puts groundwater at roughly 5 to 10 feet along Sulphur, Aliso and Oso Creeks. Shallow groundwater can push moisture up through a slab after a long wet period, so a damp floor with no visible leak is worth a moisture reading.

It also flags expansive soils, which can cause minor to severe structural damage. After a soaking, watch for new cracks near doors and slab edges, photograph them, and mention them when you call.

When to call a crew and when to handle it yourself

  • Call a crew if the water came from outside, covered more than one room, or touched drywall
  • Call a crew if the water stood more than a day, since drying clocks start fast
  • Call 911 or the utility for any gas smell or water around live electrical
  • Handle it yourself only if it is a small amount of clean water on a hard floor you can dry within hours
  • Do not enter if the garage panel or outlets are submerged
  • Do not mix cleaners or use bleach with ammonia, and avoid using household fans after 48 hours
