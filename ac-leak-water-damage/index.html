Where AC leak water damage in Laguna Hills comes from

AC leak water damage in Laguna Hills starts with condensation. An air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air as it cools it, and that water is supposed to leave through a drain line. When the path fails, the water goes into whatever sits under or beside the unit. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry reference for water categories and drying goals, and we cite it as a reference only.

The usual causes are a clogged condensate drain line, a cracked or overflowing drain pan, a frozen evaporator coil that melts when the system shuts off, and a float switch that fails to stop the unit. A ductless wall unit can also drain down the wall behind it instead of out through its hose.

Air handlers in attics and hall closets are the common trouble spots, because nobody looks at them for months. These are among the more common quiet water losses in homes with attic or closet air handlers, since a slow drip can run for weeks before a stain shows.

Safety first: the first hour

These steps keep the leak from growing while you wait for dispatch and the HVAC contractor.

  1. Turn the system off

    Set the thermostat to off. A running unit keeps making condensate, and a frozen coil will keep melting into the pan.

  2. Check the power before you touch anything

    If water is dripping near a light, outlet or the air handler's own wiring, stay back. Cut power only at a dry panel. If the panel area is wet, call 911 or the utility.

  3. Photograph before cleanup

    Take pictures of the unit, the pan, the stain and any standing water. Add a short video of the ceiling and keep the wet material.

  4. Call the right people

    Call the 24/7 line at (949) 670-0939 for the water damage, and a CSLB-licensed HVAC contractor for the unit and its drain.

How a crew handles an attic or closet leak

The crew starts with moisture meters on the ceiling, wall and floor, then scans with a thermal imaging camera to find cool, wet areas behind drywall. In an attic, that often means walking the joists and lifting insulation near the air handler and along the drain line.

Standing water is pulled out with extraction units or wet vacuums. Wet insulation is bagged and removed, and drywall that is soft or has been wet for more than 72 hours is generally cut out. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the framing, which is the in-depth work described on structural drying.

A dark attic with soaked insulation is where mold watch matters most. The crew checks the underside of the roof deck and the framing, and if growth is found, mold remediation is scoped separately.

Is an AC leak covered?

Policies typically treat a sudden, accidental release differently from water that seeped slowly over weeks, and a long condensate leak often lands in that gradual-damage question. Read your own policy and ask your insurer, because we cannot say what is covered.

The California Department of Insurance advises reporting damage promptly, photographing it, keeping receipts and waiting for the adjuster before permanent repairs. Keep the old drain line, pan or any part the HVAC contractor removes, since it shows how the leak happened.
