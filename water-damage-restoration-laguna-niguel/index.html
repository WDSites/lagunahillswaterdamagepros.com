When water is spreading in Laguna Niguel, what do I do first?

Call (949) 670-0939 and stop the source if you can. Dispatch is staffed 24/7, and the first minutes decide how much drywall, flooring and insulation can be saved. A crew goes out with same-day response when available.

Then work the list in this order: stop the flow, deal with power only if it is safe, lift what you can, keep out of contaminated water and take pictures. The cards below spell each one out.

If water is still spreading: before we arrive

Stop the water

Close the main valve or the shutoff at the fixture. For outside water, block the door gap with towels or sandbags only if it is safe to go near.

Mind the electrical

A dry panel lets you turn off breakers to wet rooms. A wet panel or standing water near outlets means 911 or the utility, not you.

Raise it

Get electronics, files, shoes and rugs onto high surfaces.

Avoid contaminated water

Flood water from outdoors and anything from a drain is Category 3. Keep family and pets out.

Record it

Capture wide shots and close-ups, then call the 24/7 line.

Who handles the water bill and the permit?

Moulton Niguel Water District lists Laguna Niguel in its service area, and the city's safety element coordinates with the district on water and fire flow. Your bill confirms it, and the district is who you call for a meter or main problem.

For repair permits, the city runs a Building and Safety function and an online permit center. Scope drives permit rules, so ask before opening walls or replacing fixtures. Remember that CSLB requires a license for any job needing a permit.

Is my Laguna Niguel home in a flood or slope area?

The city's safety element says FEMA 100-year flood zones lie in several locations and 500-year zones in a few northern areas. It does not name a creek, so check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address instead of guessing. It also says the city is not susceptible to dam failure or tsunami.

The same document says the greatest flood risks come from damaged, underengineered or obstructed infrastructure, and it has policies on slope failure, landslides and erosion, with geotechnical studies required in hazard areas. In practice, a blocked brow ditch or a crushed downspout line on your own lot is a flood problem you can prevent.

More than a third of the city is open space, so many homes sit next to slopes and canyon edges. After heavy rain, look for new wet spots at the base of a slope wall.

What does insurance cover when water gets in?

The state insurance department says a homeowners policy generally leaves out flood, so a separate NFIP policy is needed, and it starts 30 days after signing. A burst pipe is a different cause and policies typically differ, so read yours. Report damage promptly, save receipts and let the adjuster see it before permanent repairs.

Insurers have 40 calendar days to accept or deny a claim after proof of claim under 10 CCR 2695.7. The CDI hotline is 800-927-4357.

If you want a neutral reference for how drying is judged, the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration is the published one. Ask the crew to explain how moisture readings will show the structure is dry before anything is closed up.

How old is my house, and does it matter?

Laguna Niguel Corporation began planning one of California's first master-planned communities in 1959, on land called Rancho Niguel. Land sales started in 1961 in Monarch Bay and Laguna Terrace, Avco Community Developers took over the plan in 1971, and the city incorporated on December 1, 1989.

That is decades of building, so a house in one tract may have very different plumbing and roofing from one three streets away. Ask the city for permit history on older work, and have crews measure moisture instead of assuming.
