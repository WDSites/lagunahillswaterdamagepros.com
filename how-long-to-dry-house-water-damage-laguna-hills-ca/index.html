How Long Does Water Damage Take to Dry in Laguna Hills, CA?

A minor leak typically takes 1 to 3 days to dry. Bigger losses take longer, and the only honest finish line is a moisture reading.

The short answer

For a minor leak, 1 to 3 days of active drying is typical. That is an industry rule of thumb, not a standard. Larger losses, hidden water in walls, or wet subfloors can run longer.

The more important number is 24 to 48 hours. The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas and items within that window to prevent mold, and mold can begin to grow in that same period. Start drying first, then sort out the schedule.

What happens on each day

  1. Day 0: water removal

    Extraction units pull standing water, and the crew maps the damage with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera.

  2. Day 1: set equipment

    Air movers go on the wet surfaces and LGR dehumidifiers run in a closed room. Wet carpet pad, insulation and soft drywall come out.

  3. Day 2 to 3: monitor

    The crew returns daily, reads the same spots and moves or removes equipment as readings fall.

  4. Longer jobs

    Deep framing, subfloors or hardwood may need several more visits, with readings logged each time.

  5. Finish

    When every location matches the dry reference reading, equipment comes out and repair starts.

Why the equipment runs so long

Air movers

They push dry air across wet surfaces and lift moisture off the material. They are placed low along walls and across floors, not aimed at one spot.

LGR dehumidifiers

They pull the moisture out of the air so it cannot settle back. A closed room lets them work against a smaller volume of air.

Heat and airflow control

Warm, moving, dry air dries faster, but dense materials such as hardwood need gentler, slower treatment.

Daily readings

Meters tell the crew which spots lag, so equipment is moved to the slow areas instead of left in the dry ones.

What a homeowner can do while it dries

  • Keep the equipment plugged in and the doors and windows closed unless the crew says otherwise.
  • Do not turn off the dehumidifier to save noise. The loud hours are what finish the job.
  • Move small items and furniture to dry areas, and keep children and pets away from wet areas.
  • Ask for the daily readings and write them down.
  • Hold off on paint, new flooring and baseboards until the crew signs off.
  • Wait for the adjuster before permanent repairs, and keep receipts and photos.

How to know the house is actually dry

Ask the crew what reading ends the job. A good answer names a target based on a dry reference reading from an unaffected part of the home, taken on the same material.

Dry to the touch is not dry. Wood, drywall and concrete can feel fine while meters read high behind the surface. If you are told it is done, ask to see the log, and ask whether the crew works to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. Our water mitigation crews give you the readings in writing. If mold shows up anyway, mold remediation is a separate stage and a separate scope.
