Sewage Backup in Laguna Hills, CA: What to Do and Not Do

Sewage is Category 3 water, so stay out of it, skip the DIY cleanup and call for help, with 911 first if there is any electrical or gas risk.

Do these first

Stay out of the affected area and keep children and pets out too. Sewage carries contamination, so do not wade in, touch it or try to mop it up.

If water is near outlets, an appliance, or the electrical panel, or you smell gas, leave and call 911. Otherwise stop using water in the house, because every flush and sink drain can push more waste out.

Then work out whether the blockage is in your line or the main line, and call a crew.

Why sewage is treated differently

Based on IICRC S500 categories, Category 1 is clean water from a supply line or rain, Category 2 is gray water such as washer or dishwasher discharge and Category 3 is black water. Sewage and ground-surface flood water are Category 3. Category 2 water left over 48 hours can also become Category 3.

That category drives the work. Porous materials such as carpet, pad, drywall and insulation that contacted Category 3 water are generally removed rather than dried in place.

Where sewage usually comes from

Backups tend to show at the lowest drain in the house, such as a floor drain in a garage, a shower or a ground-level bathroom. Waste has nowhere else to go when the line downstream is blocked, so it comes out where gravity lets it.

Roots, grease, wipes and collapsed pipe sections cause most blockages. A single slow drain may be a local clog, while several fixtures failing together suggests the main line.

Mark where it entered and how far it spread. That detail helps both the plumber and the crew.

What not to do

  • Do not walk through the water or let children and pets near it.
  • Do not run a household wet-dry vacuum or box fans through it. They spread contamination.
  • Do not use the affected toilets, sinks, tubs or washing machine until the line is clear.
  • Do not mix bleach with other cleaners to disinfect. Never mix bleach with ammonia.
  • Do not throw away everything before photographing it for your records.
  • Do not touch electrical equipment if standing water is nearby.

Handling the cleanup yourself without protective gear and proper disposal is the riskiest option. We do not recommend DIY for sewage.

What the cleanup involves

  1. Assess and protect

    A crew puts on PPE, isolates the area and checks moisture with a meter and a thermal imaging camera.

  2. Extract

    Truck-mounted or portable extraction units remove standing contaminated water.

  3. Remove affected materials

    Carpet, pad, baseboard, and drywall that wicked contaminated water are cut out and bagged.

  4. Clean and apply antimicrobial

    Hard surfaces are washed with detergent, then treated with an antimicrobial suited to the surface.

  5. Dry and verify

    LGR dehumidifiers, air movers and HEPA air scrubbers run until readings meet the drying goal.

The broader process is laid out under flood damage restoration, and emergency water removal covers the first-response side.

Contents, floors and walls

Hard items like tile, sealed concrete and some furniture can often be cleaned and disinfected. Carpet, pad, upholstered furniture and cardboard that soaked up contaminated water are generally discarded.

Walls matter too. Drywall that wicked water upward from the floor is typically cut out above the visible line, and insulation inside is removed. Wood framing is cleaned and dried in place.

Photograph everything before it is bagged, and keep a list of items for your insurer.

After the water is gone

Drying equipment stays in place until a moisture meter shows the structure has reached its goal. That can take several days, and sewage jobs also need cleaning and disinfecting before rebuild.

Odor, staining or dampness that returns after cleanup is a sign something stayed wet. Tell the crew rather than covering it with air fresheners or paint.

Rebuild work such as new drywall and flooring comes last, once the area is clean and dry.
