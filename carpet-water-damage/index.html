Four layers, four different drying problems

Carpet water damage restoration in Laguna Hills is really a job about four stacked materials: the carpet face, the cushion pad, the tack strip along the walls, and the slab or subfloor underneath. Water that wets the top layer drops straight to the pad, which acts like a sponge and holds moisture long after the surface feels fine.

Most homes here sit on concrete slabs, so water that does not evaporate tends to sit between the pad and the slab. That is why a carpet that feels almost dry can still be wet where it matters.

How each layer behaves when wet

Carpet face

Synthetic fibers shed water well, but the backing and seams hold moisture.

Cushion pad

Pad absorbs water fast and is cheap to replace, so it is the first thing to go.

Tack strip

Wood strips along the walls soak up water and can stain the carpet edge.

Slab or subfloor

Concrete takes on water slowly and releases it slowly into the carpet above.

Safety first: the first hour with wet carpet

  1. Stop the water

    Close the supply valve at the fixture, or shut the main valve at the street side of the house if you cannot find the source.

  2. Mind the electricity

    Cut power to the room at the panel only if the floor around it is dry. Otherwise call 911 or your utility.

  3. Stay off the wet area

    Walking on soaked carpet pushes water into the pad. Put foil under furniture legs.

  4. Photograph before anything is lifted

    Shoot the carpet, the wet line on the walls and the furniture. Note the start time.

  5. Call early

    The 24/7 line is (949) 670-0939. Say where the water came from and how long it has sat.

What the crew does to dry carpet

A technician maps the wet area with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera. Extraction units pull water from the carpet and pad.

Wet pad is cut out and bagged. The carpet is lifted at a corner and floated on air movers, and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the room.

Drying continues until the moisture readings match a dry reference point in the same room. The general method is explained on the water mitigation page.

Why the slab keeps wicking after the carpet feels dry

Concrete behaves like a hard sponge. It can hold water from a leak, a storm or the ground, and it releases that moisture upward over days or weeks into any pad or carpet laid on top.

The city's adopted Safety Element says groundwater is shallow, about 5 to 10 feet, along Sulphur, Aliso and Oso Creeks. For homes in those corridors, it is worth asking whether moisture rising through the slab is part of the story, and a crew can check rather than guess. That is a possibility to test, not a diagnosis.

How a crew tests the slab

  • A concrete-rated moisture meter is pressed on the slab in several spots, including one far from the wet area for comparison.
  • A thermal imaging camera shows cool patches where water is still evaporating.
  • A taped sheet of plastic is left on the slab for a day, and moisture beneath it shows that vapor is still coming up.
  • New carpet and pad are held back until the slab readings settle.

Where carpet odors come from

Odor starts in the pad, not the carpet face. Damp foam and bacteria in the backing give off a sour, musty smell within a couple of days, and that smell comes back in humid weather when the carpet is not truly dry.

The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold and notes that carpet may need to be thrown away if it stays wet. California Department of Public Health guidance, "Mold in My Home," says porous items such as carpet are generally discarded after serious wetting, and not to run fans if it has been more than 48 hours since the flooding. Mold needs moisture, so the moisture source is fixed first.

What happens at reinstall

  1. Confirm the slab and subfloor are dry

    Readings should match the reference, and the room should have no lingering odor.

  2. Lay new pad

    A fresh pad goes down, with seams taped, and the carpet is stretched and seated.

  3. Trim and finish

    Edges are trimmed, seams are checked and the room is vacuumed. Repair work follows once the structure is dry, as covered under structural drying.

Insurance and wet carpet

Policies typically treat a sudden pipe or appliance leak differently from a slow seep or from flood water that rises from outside. Read your own policy, and ask your insurer which one fits your loss.

The California Department of Insurance advises photographing damage, keeping receipts and waiting for the adjuster before permanent repairs. Keep a swatch of the carpet and a photo of the pad label if you can, since they help with the claim. We do not decide coverage.

Mistakes that cost homeowners their carpet

  • Shampooing wet carpet and leaving the pad underneath untouched.
  • Running fans over carpet that has been wet for more than 48 hours without a professional assessment.
  • Leaving damp pad in place because the surface feels dry.
  • Replacing the carpet before the slab is confirmed dry.
