Fixing Water-Damaged Hardwood Floors in Laguna Hills, CA

Wet hardwood can often be saved if it dries slowly and fully before anyone sands or refinishes it.

The short answer: dry first, repair last

Water-damaged hardwood can often be saved, but only if the boards and the subfloor dry out first. Cupped boards, with edges higher than the center, usually mean moisture came from below. They often flatten as they dry, so do not sand or refinish until the readings say they are done.

Do not point fans or space heaters at the floor. Fast, uneven heat can crack boards and leave moisture trapped underneath. Sanding too early does the same thing, because the new finish seals wet wood.

Cupping, crowning and buckling

The shape of the damage tells you what the water did.

Cupping

Board edges rise above the center. This usually points to moisture from below, such as a slab, a leaking line or a wet crawl space.

Crowning

The center rises above the edges. It often shows up after a cupped floor was sanded flat while still wet, then dried.

Buckling

Boards lift off the subfloor, sometimes several inches. This is serious. Stop foot traffic and call a crew.

Staining and darkening

Black or gray marks that sand cannot remove suggest water or mold has been in the wood a while.

What to do in the first day

  1. Stop the water

    Shut off the supply to the fixture or the main valve. A dripping dishwasher, refrigerator line or water heater will keep soaking the boards.

  2. Remove standing water and loose items

    Mop up or have a crew run an extraction unit. Take rugs, mats and furniture off the floor, and place foil or plastic under any legs that must stay.

  3. Do not seal it in

    Pull off rugs and plastic that trap moisture. Run the air conditioner on a normal setting, but leave the fans and heaters off.

  4. Photograph and log

    Pictures of cupping, stains and the source help later with a claim.

  5. Call for moisture readings

    A crew uses moisture meters on the wood and the subfloor, and a thermal imaging camera to find wet areas you cannot see.

How a crew dries hardwood

Hardwood is dried gradually. Crews often use low-speed air movers and an LGR dehumidifier to pull moisture out of the room air, while a mat or panel system can pull water from the boards themselves in some jobs.

Readings are logged each visit on the wood and the subfloor. Drying is finished when the numbers settle to the dry reference reading, not when the floor looks flat. Per the EPA, wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to head off mold, so a slow start costs you options.

Engineered flooring behaves differently from solid planks, since its layered core can swell and delaminate. Tell the crew which one you have.

What a homeowner can do and what to leave alone

You can stop the water, soak up the surface and take photos. You should not rent a drum sander for the next afternoon, and you should not coat a damp floor with a sealer.

Call water damage repair crews when boards are lifting, when the subfloor is soft, or when the water category is unclear. Our water mitigation teams handle the dry-out and the log. We do not refinish floors that are still reading wet, and we do not do roofing or plumbing fixes.
